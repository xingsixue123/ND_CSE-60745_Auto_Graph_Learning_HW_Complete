Because every class contributes equally regardless of size, a model that ignores a rare class is
punished. This is why Macro-F1 matters: on BlogCatalog or Flickr, where label frequencies
span orders of magnitude, a model can score well on Micro-F1 while never predicting most of
the 39 (resp. 195) labels — the gap between the two numbers is the diagnostic, and reporting
only Micro-F1 hides it.
• ROC-AUC, computed one-vs-rest and averaged over classes for multi-class problems, and av-
eraged over tasks for multi-label problems (this is the ogbn-proteins metric). It is threshold-free,
so it measures ranking quality rather than the quality of a particular decision threshold.
• Reporting convention. A single number is not a result. The convention is mean ± standard
deviation over 10 (OGB, fixed split, 10 seeds) or 100 (Shchur et al., random splits)
runs, since the spread across splits on the small benchmarks routinely exceeds the differences
between published methods.
• Protocol for unsupervised embeddings. DeepWalk/LINE/node2vec/DGI are evaluated by
the linear-probe protocol: learn the embedding with no labels, freeze it, train a one-vs-rest ℓ2-
regularised logistic regression on the embeddings of a fraction ρ of nodes, and report Micro-F1
and Macro-F1 on the rest. Results are given as a curve over training ratios ρ ∈{10%, . . . , 90%}
(BlogCatalog) or {1%, . . . , 10%} (Flickr, YouTube).
