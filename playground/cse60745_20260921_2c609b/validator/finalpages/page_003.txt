Coauthor CS and Coauthor Physics come from the KDD Cup 2016 Microsoft Academic Graph:
an edge means two authors co-authored a paper, features are binary keyword indicators over an
author’s papers, and the label is the author’s most active field of study. Amazon Computers and
Amazon Photo are segments of the Amazon co-purchase graph: an edge means two products are
frequently bought together, features are binary bag-of-words over product reviews, and the label is
the product category. All four are used for node classification and, being attributed and undirected,
also serve as link prediction benchmarks.
1.3 Web-page and heterophilous benchmarks (the counterexamples)
The benchmarks above are strongly homophilous, and a GNN that simply averages over neighbour-
hoods does well on them almost by construction. The suite assembled by Pei et al. (Geom-GCN,
ICLR 2020) and popularised by Zhu et al. (Beyond Homophily in Graph Neural Networks, NeurIPS
2020) exists to break that assumption.
WebKB (Cornell, Texas, Wisconsin) are university web pages linked by hyperlinks, with binary
bag-of-words features and 5 page classes (student, project, course, staff, faculty); they are very small
and very heterophilous, so variance across splits is large and results must be averaged over the 10
standard splits. Actor is the actor-only induced subgraph of a film–director–actor–writer network,
where an edge means two actors are co-mentioned on the same Wikipedia page. Chameleon and
Squirrel are Wikipedia page–page networks on those topics, with nodes binned into 5 classes by
average monthly traffic. All six are used almost exclusively for node classification.

2(i) Which edge set. We evaluate Eq. (1) over distinct unordered pairs — precisely the set counted by the |E|
column of Table 1, so the ratio and its denominator refer to the same object. The six directed graphs also admit
a count over the stored directed entries, which weights a reciprocated arc twice as heavily as a one-way arc; that
gives slightly different values (e.g. Chameleon 0.235 rather than 0.231) and agrees less well with the literature. (ii)
Self-loops. Texas and Wisconsin carry 16 each, Actor 93 and Squirrel 140, and a self-loop trivially satisfies yu = yv,
so it always counts as homophilous. Following Zhu et al. we include them; excluding them would give Texas 0.06
rather than 0.11 and Wisconsin 0.18 rather than 0.21 — a large relative change on exactly the datasets whose
heterophily is the point. Under the stated convention our values reproduce Zhu et al.’s published figures exactly to
two decimal places for Cora, CiteSeer, PubMed, Texas, Wisconsin, Actor, Chameleon and Squirrel. Cornell is the
sole exception: Zhu et al. report 0.30 and we measure 0.13. We could not reproduce 0.30 under any definition we
tried — over unordered pairs 0.132, the same excluding self-loops 0.123, over the stored directed entries 0.131, or
node homophily in the sense of Pei et al. 0.186 — so we report the measured value and flag the disagreement rather
than repeating a figure we cannot reproduce.
