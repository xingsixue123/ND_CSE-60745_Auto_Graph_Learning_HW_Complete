Q4(c): Degree distribution of the whole graph (10 pts)
Figure 4 plots the degree distribution of the entire graph (all 26,728 nodes, not just the giant
component). The left panel gives the raw node count N(k) on linear axes; the right panel gives
the fraction of nodes P(k) = N(k)/n on log–log axes, which is the informative view for a sparse
graph with a heavy tail.
Answer. The degree ranges from a minimum of 1 to a maximum of 59, with mean degree
1.974 (= 2m/n = 2 × 26,377/26,728). No node is isolated, since every node enters the graph
through an edge.
The distribution is strongly right-skewed. Degree 1 alone accounts for 13,942 nodes (52.2%) and
degree 2 for a further 7,423 (27.8%), so four fifths of the graph has degree at most 2; the median
degree is 1. Only 37 distinct degree values occur at all, and the tail is thin and ragged — the
largest degrees (36, 38, 40, 51, 57, 59) are each realised by a single node, the maximum being
