Construction.
The graph was built in NetworkX by creating an empty undirected graph,
adding the ten nodes 0, . . . , 9, and then adding the fifteen edges listed above:
G = nx.Graph()
G.add_nodes_from(range(10))
G.add_edges_from(E)
Checks on the transcription.
Every vertex has degree exactly 3, so the graph is 3-regular
and the handshake lemma is satisfied: ∑︁
v deg(v) = 30 = 2|E| = 2 · 15. The graph is connected,
has no triangles, and has girth 5.
In fact these fifteen edges reproduce the Petersen graph
(checked with NetworkX’s is_isomorphic against petersen_graph(), which returns True),
the standard 3-regular, girth-5 graph on ten vertices. Landing on the Petersen graph is a strong
confirmation that the edge list was read off the drawing correctly.
One edge deserves comment, because the drawing in the assignment is misleading at that point:
vertices 5, 4 and 8 are very nearly collinear, and the single edge (5, 8) is drawn passing straight
across the disc of vertex 4. It therefore looks like a pair of edges (5, 4) and (4, 8), but it is one
edge; if it were two, vertices 5, 4 and 8 would all have degree 4 and the graph would not be
3-regular. In Figure 1 the edge (5, 8) is drawn with a slight bow so that it visibly passes clear
of vertex 4.
