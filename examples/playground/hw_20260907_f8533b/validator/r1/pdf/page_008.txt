At L2 the pair of techniques removes 31.25% of the miss service time (50 CCs); at L1 it removes
only 10% (3 CCs). The gap is even sharper for critical-word first taken on its own: at L2 it improves
on early restart alone (118 →110 CCs), whereas at L1 it makes matters worse (24 →27 CCs),
because its 27 −18 = 9 CC surcharge exceeds the 6 CC that early restart had already saved. So at
L1 it would be better not to enable critical-word first at all, while at L2 it pays for itself — which
is precisely what “more important for L2” means.
Factors that drive the relative importance.
1. Words per block, n. The most these techniques can ever save is the transfer tail (n −1)tx
— the time spent shipping the words the processor is not waiting for. Larger blocks mean
a longer tail and more to skip. Lower cache levels normally use larger blocks (8 words here
versus 4), which favours L2.
