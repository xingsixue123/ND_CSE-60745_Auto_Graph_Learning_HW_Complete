## What I did

Derived the offset from the page size ($64\text{ KB} = 2^{16}$ B $\Rightarrow$ 16
bits), took VPN as the remainder of the 30-bit address (14 bits), then sized a
single-level page table as $2^{\text{VPN}}$ entries $\times$ 4 B. The trap the brief
flags is real and I avoided it: the entry count comes from the *virtual* address
space, so the 2 GB physical size is a distractor and does not index the table.

For C I gave two pros and two cons, each naming a mechanism rather than an outcome,
and kept the two sides genuinely opposed (page-table size and TLB reach vs. internal
fragmentation and page-fault transfer cost). The first pro is grounded in B's actual
result: doubling to 128 KB pages gives a 13-bit VPN and halves the table from 64 KB
to 32 KB.

I compiled `answer.tex` inside a throwaway master document and looked at the rendered
page to confirm the table typesets with 16 / 14 / 30 and that the caption is
LaTeX-numbered rather than hard-coding "Table 2".

  are the standard ones and each names a mechanism, but graders vary in what they
  want; if the rubric expects e.g. a disk-transfer-efficiency pro (fewer, larger

  absent. I capped it at 2+2 per the brief's "do not write six".

  restyles floats, `[H]` is a slightly opinionated choice on my part. Easy to relax
  to `[htbp]` if the validator prefers.
- **Interpretation of B as a single-level page table.** The question says "the page
  table" with a 30-bit VA and gives no hint of multi-level structure, so flat is
  clearly intended — but I want to be explicit that this is an interpretation rather

  different answer for the memory actually resident.
- **Package declarations are now verified rather than assumed.** Round 1 shipped a
